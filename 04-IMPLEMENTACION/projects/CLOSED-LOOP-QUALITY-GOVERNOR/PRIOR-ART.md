# IDEA I-08 — CLOSED-LOOP QUALITY GOVERNOR: PRIOR ART

Novelty audit. Sources ranked primary > community. The subject decomposes into (A) closed-loop quality/resolution control in shipped games & engines, (B) adaptive rendering/power research, (C) GPU DVFS/governor feedback control, (D) the "no counters, read-only signals" angle, (E) how that maps to Polaris12.

## 1. DYNAMIC RESOLUTION AS A QUALITY GOVERNOR — SHIPPED, PRIMARY SOURCES
1. **Consoles — Diablo III on Xbox One X / PS4 Pro (Digital Foundry, 2017)** — shipped dynamic-resolution governor: X1X uses a 73.2 % resolution scale (3648×2052) vs PS4P 66.7 % (2880×1620), modulated per-frame based on measured frame budget. **Proves the closed-loop DRS governor is standard console technology.** https://www.digitalfoundry.net/articles/digitalfoundry-2017-diablo-3-ultra-hd-xbox-one-x-ps4-pro-dynamic-res-showdown
2. **Intel Dynamic Resolution Rendering whitepaper + sample** — the canonical PC implementation: viewport-scale render targets (from subsample to supersample), scale delta proportional to dimensionless (desired−current) frame time, UI at backbuffer res, "the control scheme is basic: a resolution scale delta is calculated proportionally to the dimensionless difference in the desired frame time and the current frame time." Directly the control-law this idea re-derives. http://lwhozo.zqwzjdq.vgamuseum.info/intel-dynamic-resolution-rendering-whitepaper.pdf ; https://www.intel.com/content/dam/develop/external/us/en/documents/dynamicresolutionrendering-183334.pdf
3. **Unity — ScalableBufferManager + FrameTimingManager (official manual)** — engine-native dynamic resolution; `FrameTimingManager` gives CPU+GPU frame times; docs literally show the governor sketch ("use FrameTimingManager to detect when GPU performance starts to decrease… calculate a new width/height scale"). https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/DynamicResolution-control.html
4. **Unity HDRP `HDDynamicResolution` (source)** — the shipped controller: **avg GPU frametime over EvaluationFrameCount frames; require ScaleUpDuration/ScaleDownDuration consecutive evaluations before stepping scale by 1/ScaleUp(ScaleDown)StepCount**. That is precisely the hysteresis + step-size + settle-time design a Polaris12 governor would mirror. https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.6/api/Global%20Namespace.HDDynamicResolution.html ; https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/blob/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition/Runtime/RenderPipeline/HDDynamicResolution.cs
5. **Unity-Technologies/DynamicResolutionSample** — reference controller logic exposed (`DesiredFrameRate`, `MinScaleFactor`, `MaxScaleFactor`, headroom∈[0.06,0.5], counters per scale-up, aggressive-down/conservative-up policy). Practical tuning knobs + pseudocode. https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/DynamicResolutionSample/
6. **Unreal Engine Dynamic Resolution** — native engine DRS (`r. DynamicRes.ResolutionPercent`), driven by GPU time. (dev.epicgames.com documentation; page returned 403 on fetch — cite as known, content not re-verified this session.) https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/dynamic-resolution-in-unreal-engine
7. **Unity Adaptive Performance (mobile)** — Indexer + Scalers: bottleneck detection (CPU/GPU/FillRate-bound from `gpuFrameTime`), **scaler targets tied to bottleneck** (resolution scaler targets GPU/FillRate), controller acts only on confirmed bottleneck. The "act on the right bottleneck" mechanism is explicit, shipped, and documented. https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.adaptiveperformance@2.2/manual/user-guide.html

## 2. ADAPTIVE RENDERING / PERCEPTUAL PRIOR ART (academic)
8. **He et al. — "Optimizing Smartphone Power Consumption through Dynamic Resolution Scaling" (ACM MobiCom 2015)** — per-frame DRS governors on Galaxy S5/Nexus 6; 30.1 % avg (60.5 % max) energy reduction per frame at half res; **runs transparently to legacy apps** by intercepting the GLES pipeline — i.e. a true OS/API-level governor, the closest thing to "works without app modification." https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2789168.2790117 ; https://people.csail.mit.edu/songtao/p27-heA.pdf
9. **Nixon et al. — "Mobile GPU power consumption reduction via dynamic resolution and frame rate scaling" (USENIX HotPower 2014)** — DRS + frame-rate scaling as a power governor on mobile GPUs. Accumulated in the MobiCom reference list; precedent for joint resolution+rate control. (cited inside #8)
10. **Jindal et al. — "Perceptual model for adaptive local shading and refresh rate" (ACM SIGGRAPH Quantif. 2021)** — content-adaptive trade-offs of resolution/refresh-rate under a perceptual budget; engine-plugin form. Shows perceptual-quality-driven adaptive control as an active research axis. https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3478513.3480514
11. **Denes et al. — "A perceptual model of motion quality for rendering with adaptive refresh-rate and resolution" (ACM TOG 2020)** — joint adaptive refresh/resolution under budget; the research background Unity/UE DRS approximates in practice. (referenced in #10)
12. **Liu / March / Mantiuk (CamLab 2026) — "Streaming of rendered content with adaptive frame rate and resolution"** — NN-predicts resolution+refresh selection (Viterbi over 2 s windows, 0.25 JOD quality budget → 53 % pixel savings) for streaming. Current-state academic adaptive-frame-quality control. https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10995v1 ; https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rkm38/pdfs/liu2026_adaptive_streaming.pdf
13. **STeFAS — "Spatio-Temporally-Filtered Adaptive Shading" (CESCG 2020)** — VRS-based spatial shading-rate governor (Turing+, no GCN4 relevance for HW-VRS, but shows the "adapt quality per frame to a budget" design pattern). https://cescg.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Stappen-Spatio-Temporally-Filtered-Adaptive-Shading-and-a-Generalized-Shading-Rate-Stabilization-Technique-1.pdf

## 3. GPU DVFS / FRAME-RATE GOVERNORS — FEEDBACK CONTROL PRIOR ART
14. **Android GPUFreq governors (survey paper)** — the exact governor vocabulary: On-demand / Conservative / Performance / Powersave, sampling interval, up-threshold, freq-step; **utilization-based feedback** to pick a clock. The "governor with utilization feedback" idea is decades old for CPU/GPU DVFS. https://www.shihada.com/node/publications/GPUFreq.pdf
15. **GreenLLM (2025)** — SLO-aware GPU DVFS with a **dual-loop controller**: coarse band selection (TPS), fine 15 MHz steps every 20 ms, **hysteresis ("update only if TPS stays in new bucket 3 consecutive windows")** to prevent jitter. Modern concrete example of hysteresis-in-DVFS-governor design. https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16449v1
16. **V-SYNC deadline-aware GPU DVFS governor (ISQED 2026)** — binds GPU frequency control to the actual present deadline (vsync event); "delayed deadline" widens the budget when needed; uses Perfetto + hardware counters at ms scale on Pixel 8 Pro; −37 % FPS variation. Shows the state-of-the-art actuator (DVFS via hardware counters) the RX550 idea *cannot* use (counters/writes prohibited) — important contrast. https://www.isqed.org/English/Proceedings/pdf/5B-1-109.pdf
17. **FC-GPU — "Feedback Control GPU Scheduling for real-time Embedded Systems" (ACM TECS 2025)** — MIMO feedback controller on NVIDIA RTX 3090 + AMD MI-100: response-time→task-rate adaptation with provable stability from control theory. Formalizes the closed-loop GPU-control framework (stability analysis via MIMO state-space). https://srinivasans74.github.io/assets/pdf/3761812.pdf
18. **SSMDVFS (DATE 2025)** — microsecond-scale DVFS on GPGPUs with supervised/self-calibrated ML; RL-based governor (DRQN, AICAS 2023) for rendering apps on Jetson (=ML-driven closed-loop GPU governor). Shows governor sophistication has moved far beyond a simple proportional law. https://doi.org/10.23919/date64628.2025.10992871 ; https://doi.org/10.1109/aicas57966.2023.10168668 ; PCSTALL predictor paper (arXiv 2205.00121) for fine-grain DVFS prediction.

## 4. CLOUD-GAMING / DRIVER-LEVEL ADAPTIVE QUALITY (adjacent prior art)
19. **Adrenaline (arXiv 2024)** — adaptive rendering-quality (RQ) optimization for cloud gaming: FPS-threshold priority, presets, and **exponential-backoff to stop oscillating RQ adjustments** — the oscillation problem is explicitly identified and solved in published systems. https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19446v1
20. **Stimpack (NSDI'26)** — adaptive rendering-quality engine for scalable cloud gaming (VMAF-predicted quality, per-user RQ). https://github.laiyagushi.com/gt-stimpack/Stimpack
21. **AMD patent — "Game Frame Classification for Adaptive GPU Driver Rendering"** — a *driver-level* classifier of game stage (menu/loading/active) to adjust rendering/present settings; i.e. driver-side adaptive rendering has been patented by vendors. Contrast for the RX550 (driver modification is out of scope). https://eureka.patsnap.com/triz-case/game-frame-driver-rendering
22. **NeuroPace-RDNA (community, 2026)** — Ring-3 telemetry-fusion closed-loop agent (ETW + ADLX) for frametime stabililty on RDNA; community-scale example of "user-space governor from read-only telemetry" — but reads ETW/ADLX hardware telemetry (unavailable on GFX8). https://github.com/l1ve709/neuropace-rdna

## 5. THE "NO COUNTERS, READ-ONLY SIGNALS" ANGLE
- Intel DRR (#2), Unity HDRP (#3/#4), UE (#6) and the console/mobile governors (#1, #8) all close their loop on **GPU/CPU frame time** (available from DX/Vulkan timers or engine timing), **not** on hardware PERF_SEL counters. So "governor from frametime without HW counters" is the **established** norm, not a gap.
- What the RX550 *addition* would be: feed the loop with **sysfs read-only telemetry** (power1_input watts, DPM clock states read-back, gpu_busy, mem_info) in a deliberately counter-free, write-free setup, on GCN4. The control design remains the shipped proportional+hysteresis pattern.
- Novelty of that packaging alone is low (the signals are the same ones radeontop already exposes); the value is **educational/measurement** value on a real low-end GCN4 part, not a new algorithm.

## 6. NOVELTY TAG ASSESSMENT
The *generic* formulation — "closed-loop quality governor with frametime feedback" — is **KNOWN**: shipped in consoles (#1), Intel (#2), Unity HDRP/UE (#3–#6), adapted in research (#8–#13), and its oscillation/hysteresis/step-size machinery is textbook (#4, #15, #19).

The *specific* RX550 framing — "same governor, but actuated only through a controllable render scale and fed **only** counter-free read-only sysfs telemetry, no writes" — is a **KNOWN VARIANT**:
- every algorithmic element is public and shipped;
- the "no HW counters / frametime-only feedback" claim is already the norm in shipped governors (contra the RFI framing that implied counters were required);
- the only delta is target platform (GCN4 low-end) + the write-free constraint (which removes the DVFS hardware-counters actuator class shown in #16, and thereby bounds the achievable control authority).

**Not novel:** the controller (proportional frametime error → stepped scale with hysteresis). **Marginal novelty:** the constrained observability-only GCN4 demonstration.

Recommended status: **C (known, worth documenting/measuring on this specific GPU)**, with the framing that it is an engineering/measurement exercise rather than a novel contribution — unless a genuinely new technique arises (e.g. using power1_input smoothness as the primary feedback on GCN4, which is not in the classic literature; would be PARTIALLY NOVEL if it shows a measurable advantage over frametime-only).
